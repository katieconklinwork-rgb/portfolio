NaturalRockShop is a family business, and that's how I got involved. It's a handcrafted silver and gold gemstone jewelry shop on Etsy, with a following built over 13 years on the platform: 17,300+ completed sales, a 4.9-star rating across more than 6,600 reviews, and nearly 1,600 items in the shop. I help maintain and refresh its presence, from the photography to the day-to-day work of running the shop.

It's a different kind of challenge than a brand built from scratch: the trust and reputation were already there, and my job was to give the listings a stronger visual identity and smoother day-to-day operations to match.

My role

The face of the brand, in person.

This job is sales, in person. At a booth or in a store, I'm the one people talk to: I answer their questions and help them find the right product. That face-to-face work is what makes a setup feel welcoming and worth stopping at.

Flwr Grls (formerly Cannababes) is a brand ambassador role in the cannabis wellness space — a trained, trusted presence in person, not a desk job. It's a hybrid role with the company's Detroit, MI base and its New York branch. Onboarding included completing official training programs for partners like Jeeter (a cannabis brand) and Planet Nugg (a wellness-focused dispensary in Farmingdale, NY), and ongoing work involves regularly submitting content (photos and check-ins) through each partner's internal channels for approval and posting, keeping everything compliant with how each partner needs to be represented.

The best part of the role is the in-person events — Unity Fest, the Long Island Cannabis Coalition's festival, was a standout, where I represented Planet Nugg directly with attendees alongside their team: engaging with customers, answering questions, and putting a face to the brand rather than just a feed. I've also worked in-store for Jeeter, including their "Mile High" pop-ups at Strain Stars in Farmingdale and Riverhead and at a street fair in Astoria. That in-person energy is what the role is really about — sales and customer engagement happen face to face first, with content supporting it rather than replacing it.

I started Purple Alert because Spring Hill needed a place that actually felt like us — somewhere students could laugh at the same inside jokes, keep up with what was happening around campus, and feel part of one community.

What began as a fun, unofficial meme page grew into something the whole campus paid attention to: news, events, weather, shout-outs, and the occasional purple badger. It was for Badgers, by Badgers — and it caught on with students, alumni, families, and even the wider Mobile community.

The account you see here started in September 2023. The original version, which I started in 2022, was deleted, so I rebuilt it from scratch.

A few hours, start to finish.

About

Hi, I'm Katherine.

Most people call me Katie. Here's a closer look at where I've been, what I care about, and everything I bring to the table beyond the résumé.

My story

Who I am.

I'm from Long Island, New York, and graduated from Garden City High School in 2020. Before that, I spent years behind the counter of my dad's craft store, Ace Handicraft, helping families pick out craft and jewelry supplies — my first real experience teaching product usage and fostering creativity in a community space.

College took me five years. I started at Loyola University Maryland in fall 2020 — fully online, straight into COVID — then moved to a hybrid format that spring, splitting time between Baltimore classrooms and a screen. After two years there, I transferred to Spring Hill College in Mobile, Alabama, where I'd stay for three years and where everything really clicked.

At Spring Hill I couldn't pick just one lane, so I built my own: a customized interdisciplinary degree combining Elementary Education and Communications. In the classroom I was student-teaching; online I was running Purple Alert, the campus account I founded that grew into something the whole school followed. Somewhere between lesson plans and memes, I realized they're the same skill: earning someone's attention, then giving them something worth understanding.

Creativity is the strength I bring into everything — I love designing lessons and finding unique ways to engage learners, especially through technology. My mom is a special education home instruction teacher, and I think that's part of why accessibility means so much to me: I see technology as a bridge to independence, and I want to help young learners realize their full potential.

Right now I'm back in New York doing social media and creative work for real brands, chasing photography and music on the side, and looking for the place where communication and education actually meet.
